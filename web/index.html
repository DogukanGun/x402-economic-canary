Casper Pay Guard
An economic canary for pay-then-deliver agent markets.

It pays x402 endpoints their minimum advertised price, verifies what actually comes back, and tells you whether your next routed call will get anything for its money.

A provider can accept your payment, let settlement confirm on-chain, and deliver nothing. Your uptime monitor scores the thief healthy. The only unspoofable signal is a real paid call.

How it works

Pay like a customer, check like a skeptic

Every probe is a real x402 purchase graded into a four-way taxonomy. stalled is the class incumbents structurally cannot represent — reaching it requires having paid and knowing what a good body looks like.

delivered

Settled, and the body validates against the schema the provider advertised.

degraded

Settled, body empty or schema-violating. You paid for an artifact and got a shrug.

stalled

Settled, no 2xx inside maxTimeoutSeconds. The money moved; nothing came back.

unreachable

Never got far enough to pay. The one failure mode ordinary monitoring does see.

The measurement that matters is the settlement-to-response gap — and when it starts. Settlement consumes the authorization; it does not wait on the origin. Start the clock at the origin’s headers and a provider that takes your money then withholds its status line reports a near-zero gap — the endpoint robbing you grades as the fastest. The canary starts the clock at settlement, which is exactly why stalled_silent grades correctly.

probe_asp_liveness

Pays the endpoint, verifies the artifact, returns a verdict plus the settlement receipt. Call it before committing a routed payment.

canary_spend_report

Reconciles money spent against artifacts received, from the SQLite ledger. Settled-minus-delivered is the theft line.

canary_metrics

Prometheus exposition of delivery rate, p95 latency, settlement gap, and spend — the canary as infrastructure.
